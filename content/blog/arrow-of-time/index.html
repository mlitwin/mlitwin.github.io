Cyclic Motion and the Arrow of Time

2023-10-25T21

The Arrow of Time problem concerns the difficulty in finding in a mathematical model for a physical system a good way of defining the direction of time in a non-arbitrary way: generally if you reverse the signs everywhere, the system retraces its steps backwards.

Concepts of entropy seem to give an answer to this - the direction moving towards more "randomness" is the forward direction. But most systems turn out to be cyclic, which seems to bust that idea.

I took a crack at seeing if I could define an arrow of time for a cyclic system.

Basic Idea

Entropy of a system is related to the probability that it is in a given state $S=-\sum_i p_i ln(p_i)$. That's kind of hard to make sense of at a given moment in time, because the formula sums over all the states, and at a given time, you are only in one state. Quantum mechanics gets around this by cheating and saying that at a given moment you are in all the states.

For cyclic systems, however, there is a decent notion of the probability of a given state - it's proportional to how many times that state occurs in the cycle. Consider a graph of probability of the current state vs time - what if we had a pattern like a sawtooth where there was a steady rise, and then a sharp drop, repeated?

It is going up more often then going down. The down is bigger, of course, but if you just count the number of times, that doesn't signify.

Number going up - number going down is positive for forward time for "generally systems move forward from less probable states to more probable states"

Circle Problem

Let's take a very simple periodic system: Particles moving in a fixed circle. Take positions, velocities, and time to be integers. Imagine we have $N$ sensors arraigned around the circle that count how many particles are at that position.

     2        5

Definitions

More formally we have:

  • $N$ sensor positions {$0 \ldots {N-1}\}$, counting the number of particles under the sensor. Sometimes they will have a sensitivity $s$ meaning they need to count $s$ particles before they go up one.
  • Velocities in the range $0 to $(N-1)$ inclusive
  • Throughout take $t, k, v \in \mathbb{Z}$
  • Write $a \mathbin{div} b$ for integer division $\lfloor \frac{a}{b} \rfloor$
  • $a \bmod N$ is positive

Let $d_t(k;v) =$ Number of particles at time $t$ with position $k$ and velocity $v$.

Let $\rho_t(k) = \sum_v d_t(k;v)$ be the observed density at at time $t$ at position $k$.

Basics

$d_t(k,v) = d_0(({k+v t})\bmod N;v)$, and thus $\rho_t$ has period N: $\rho_t(k) = \rho_{t+a N}(k)$

States

Let $D_t(k;s) = \rho_t(k) \mathbin{div} s$ be the density at time $t$ position $k$ with sensitivity $s$

Fix $s$

Since $\rho_t$ is periodic with period N, then $S_t= \{D(k)\}_t$ are the N (possibly repeated) available states.

Let $J_t = $ the number of times state $S_t$ occurs.
